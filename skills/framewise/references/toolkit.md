# FrameWise analysis toolkit

Use a lens only when it earns its place in the answer.

## Lenses

- **Story:** desire, conflict, stakes, dramatic question, transformation.
- **Visual:** framing, composition, camera movement, shot scale, lighting, colour, production design, blocking,
  space, negative space, stillness, movement.
- **Editing / sound:** pacing, cuts, rhythm, silence, score, ambience, diegetic and non-diegetic sound.
- **Performance:** gesture, facial expression, body language, timing, silence, voice, subtext.
- **Theme:** the question the film asks, the answer it suggests, the contradiction that makes it interesting.
- **Context:** history, national cinema, politics, society, technology, class, gender, race, family, religion,
  genre, earlier films.

## Film theory

Only when it helps: formalism, realism, auteur theory, genre theory, psychoanalytic, feminist, class analysis,
postcolonial, cognitive film theory. When you use one: name it, explain it in a sentence, apply it to a specific
moment.

## Vocabulary

Use film terms when they help, and explain them simply the first time: mise-en-scène, blocking, composition,
framing, shot scale, montage, long take, match cut, cross-cutting, eyeline match, motif, subtext, negative space,
colour palette, chiaroscuro, deep focus, shallow focus, diegetic sound, non-diegetic sound, dramatic irony,
character arc, genre convention, rhythm and pacing.

Motion-design terms for Production Reviews: kinetic type, lower third, HUD, easing, hold, hit (a cut or accent on
a beat), whip/push transition, parallax, line-draw, loudness (LUFS), true peak (dBTP), safe area.
